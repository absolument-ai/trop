# TROP Style DNA — annotated

This file explains *why* TROP prompts work, beat by beat, so the flavor can be recreated on any brief without copying a single original.

## 1. Two dissections

### "Direct Flash" (original 1.1), annotated

| Beat | Original wording | What it teaches |
|---|---|---|
| Frame | "A raw, high-impact editorial photograph" | 2 charged adjectives + medium + genre (editorial). Sets the stance immediately. |
| Hero | "a rebellious youth in a glossy black vinyl trench coat" | Character + one precise material. Vinyl = reflects flash = the light will "bite". |
| Light | "harsh 35mm point-and-shoot direct camera flash" | The light names a *device* and an *era*, not just a direction. |
| World | "gritty, stained concrete wall in a dark alleyway" | Texture with history (stains). |
| Light as layout | "…leaving a natural void for typography" | Light clears space for the word. Composition is designed, not left to chance. |
| Palette | "violently saturated… realistic, unpolished texture" | Intensity adverb + anti-polish note. |
| Word | `"PULSE 2026"` in "bold, sterile white sans-serif" in the corner | Clinical type vs. raw image = tension. Time-stamp = trend-report now-ness. |
| Fingerprint | "found-photo", "subtle red-eye", "slight lens flare" | Imperfections that prove a real camera. |
| Why | "lived-in, anti-studio, authentically human… before any message is processed" | Definition by negation + viewer timing. |
| Params | `--ar 4:5` | Editorial / social portrait format. |

### "Sensory Storytelling" (original 1.3), annotated

| Beat | Original wording | What it teaches |
|---|---|---|
| Frame | "An intimate, tactile macro photograph designed to evoke an immediate physical sensation before a single word is read" | The intent is *in* the frame sentence. |
| Hero | "heavy, condensation-covered emerald glass" | Weight + temperature + gem-color. Three senses in five words. |
| World | "incredibly soft, cream-colored pleated silk" | Soft vs. cold glass = tactile tension. |
| Light | "hazy, diffused morning glow that suggests warmth and comfort" | Light → emotion, explicitly. |
| Fingerprint | "crisp water droplets frozen in detail… 'feel' the coldness" | Scare quotes on the sensory verb. |
| Word | `"BREATHE"`, "thin, elegant, widely-spaced serif", lower third | Typography mirrors the mood (quiet), placement given. |
| Why | "a whisper, not a shout… a slight pause and a deep emotional connection" | Paradox pair + viewer effect. |

**Lesson:** loud trends and quiet trends share the same skeleton. Only the dials change.

## 2. The dials

Every beat is a dial. A TROP prompt sets them deliberately:

- **Volume**: whisper ←→ shout (Sensory vs. Skate Shop)
- **Order**: catalog grid ←→ total collage (Index vs. Maximalism)
- **Reality**: documentary ←→ impossible (Americana vs. Surrealism)
- **Hand**: machine-clean ←→ visibly handmade (Index vs. Scribbles)
- **Era**: archaic ←→ futuristic (Future Medieval spans both)
- **Distance**: macro intimacy ←→ fisheye aggression

Choosing a new combination of dial positions is how you invent a trend.

## 3. Vocabulary families (inspiration, not a checklist)

Use these as *families* to find your own words. Do not paste the same terms in every prompt.

- **Frame adjectives**: raw, high-impact, hyper-dense, intimate, tactile, dynamic, gritty, high-concept, quiet, narratively heavy, meticulously ordered, loud, dark, mystical, fine-art.
- **Materials**: vinyl, velvet, faux fur, enamel, pleated silk, chrome, rough-hewn wood, peeling wallpaper, newsprint, wax crayon, tech-wear, obsidian, polaroid, scotch tape.
- **Light sources**: point-and-shoot flash, morning haze, golden hour, invisible multiple sources, flat overhead, tungsten bulb, north window, neon spill.
- **Color as matter**: obsidian, molten orange, acid green, electric blue, burnt sienna, sage, faded ochre, canary yellow, acid violet, electric cyan, emerald, cream.
- **Fingerprints**: film grain, scratches, chemical artifacts, light leaks, scanner dust, motion drag, halftone, ink bleed, off-register print, chromatic aberration, red-eye, lens flare, JPEG noise, staple holes, coffee ring.
- **Type treatments**: liquified chrome, circular warp along the lens, distressed typewriter, ink-smudged, geometric blackletter, brutalist block, sterile sans, widely-spaced serif, mismatched oversized fonts, frantic handwritten scrawl, technical labels ("FIG. 04").
- **Why-verbs**: forces the viewer to linger, pulls the audience into, rewards the viewer for, creates a pause, sparks curiosity, signals humanity, reconnects with.

## 4. Generic vs. TROP

**Generic prompt**
> A beautiful cup of coffee on a table, cozy atmosphere, warm lighting, 8k, ultra detailed, masterpiece.

**TROP prompt**
> A slow, smoke-heavy editorial photograph built to be smelled before it is seen. A chipped enamel mug, still steaming, sits on the scarred steel counter of an all-night roadside diner at 4 a.m. Behind it, a rain-streaked window bleeds the red neon of a motel sign into soft, wet bokeh. A single tungsten bulb carves a warm cone of light around the mug, letting the rest of the room sink into a velvety brown dark that leaves the upper third empty. Rising from that darkness, the word "AWAKE" is set in a tall, condensed slab serif, cream-colored and slightly blurred as if seen through the steam. The palette is roasted umber, oxblood red and tobacco gold. Fine 400-ISO grain and a faint fingerprint smudge on the lens keep it honest. It is a quiet ritual, not a product shot — an image that makes the viewer warm their hands before they read a word. --ar 4:5 --v 7

What changed: a stance (ritual, not product shot), a material hero (chipped enamel), a world with history (all-night diner), light that composes (cone + empty upper third), a word in tension with the dark, colors as matter, a fingerprint, a closing why.

## 5. Rhythm

- Sentences of mixed length. One long descriptive sentence, then a short verdict.
- Present tense, declarative. No "please", no "I want".
- The last sentence lands like a tagline.

# TROP Trend Lexicon

Each trend is a **lever sheet**: what it refuses, and the dials it turns. Combine freely. Never rebuild an original prompt from these sheets — pick new heroes, new worlds, new words.

---

## Photographic trends

### Direct Flash
- **Refuses**: studio polish, flattering light.
- **Heroes**: people caught mid-life, reflective materials (vinyl, sequins, wet skin, metal).
- **Worlds**: alleys, parking lots, kitchens at night, party corners, stairwells.
- **Light**: on-camera flash, hard falloff into black, specular hot spots.
- **Type**: clean, clinical sans to contrast the rawness; placed in the dark void.
- **Fingerprints**: red-eye, flash burn, lens flare, date stamp, found-photo framing.
- **Ratios**: 4:5, 3:2.

### Maximalism
- **Refuses**: white space, restraint, "clean".
- **Heroes**: an object or word drowned in a curated mess.
- **Worlds**: collage fields, cabinets of curiosities, overloaded interiors.
- **Light**: everywhere at once, glossy highlights on every surface.
- **Type**: 3D, chrome, liquified, inflated, center stage.
- **Palette**: extreme saturation, clashing complementaries.
- **Fingerprints**: glitch patterns, clippings, layered prints; *hidden hierarchy* inside chaos.
- **Ratios**: 1:1, 4:5.

### Sensory Storytelling
- **Refuses**: shouting, explaining.
- **Heroes**: one tactile object with temperature, weight or moisture.
- **Worlds**: fabric, skin, stone, water, steam, paper — macro scale.
- **Light**: diffused, hazy, time-of-day specific.
- **Type**: thin, elegant, widely tracked, discreet placement.
- **Fingerprints**: droplets, fibers, condensation, shallow depth of field.
- **Ratios**: 4:5, 1:1.

### Distorted Wide Angle
- **Refuses**: static, polite framing.
- **Heroes**: bodies in motion very close to the lens (hands, shoes, faces).
- **Worlds**: architecture that can bend — towers, stairs, tunnels, crowds.
- **Light**: hard daylight or club light; grainy film look.
- **Type**: warped along the lens curvature.
- **Fingerprints**: barrel distortion, vignetting, motion blur, 90s music-video vibe.
- **Ratios**: 16:9, 9:16.

### Grunge
- **Refuses**: hyper-polished commercial beauty.
- **Heroes**: people with visible dirt, sweat, scars, honest bodies; worn objects.
- **Worlds**: basements, garages, rehearsal rooms, back rooms.
- **Light**: low, practical, single bulb, often B&W.
- **Type**: distressed, typewriter, smudged ink, stencil.
- **Fingerprints**: heavy grain, scratches, chemical stains, analog damage.
- **Ratios**: 4:5, 3:2.

### Surrealism
- **Refuses**: the literal, the obvious.
- **Heroes**: one familiar figure with one impossible element.
- **Worlds**: vast empty spaces (salt flats, pools, corridors) or banal rooms gone wrong.
- **Light**: uncanny, multiple invisible sources, wrong shadows.
- **Type**: floating, liquid, half-submerged, defying physics.
- **Fingerprints**: reflections that disagree, gravity reversed.
- **Ratios**: 1:1, 4:5.

### Americana
- **Refuses**: romanticized nostalgia.
- **Heroes**: weathered objects and people of the land.
- **Worlds**: prairies, roadsides, small towns, motels, gas stations, ranches.
- **Light**: golden hour, dust in the air, long shadows.
- **Type**: heavy, rigid, brutalist blocks against natural texture.
- **Palette**: burnt earth tones, sage, faded ochres.
- **Ratios**: 16:9, 3:2.

## Design trends

### Scrapbook / Scanner
- **Refuses**: pixel-perfection.
- **Heroes**: paper, stamps, polaroids, tickets, notes.
- **Worlds**: colored paper beds with fiber texture.
- **Type**: dragged across a flatbed scanner, light leaks, motion blur.
- **Fingerprints**: tape, jagged cuts, overlapping shadows, scanner dust, mistakes left in.
- **Ratios**: 4:5, 1:1.

### Cyber Goth
- **Refuses**: softness, pastel safety.
- **Heroes**: chrome spikes, digital tribal line work, armored silhouettes.
- **Worlds**: obsidian void with digital noise.
- **Type**: angular, sharp, iridescent silver.
- **Palette**: black + toxic hyper-colors (acid violet, electric cyan).
- **Fingerprints**: chromatic aberration, distortion, noise. The design has "teeth".
- **Ratios**: 1:1, 9:16.

### Future Medieval
- **Refuses**: tech-focused minimalism.
- **Heroes**: modern figures with ornamental, engraved, heraldic detailing.
- **Worlds**: misty forests, cathedrals, strict symmetry, mythical creatures.
- **Type**: geometric blackletter, archaic and digital at once.
- **Fingerprints**: glowing engravings, ornamental borders; "a mythical artifact from now".
- **Ratios**: 4:5, 2:3.

### Skate / Surf Shop
- **Refuses**: grids and structure.
- **Heroes**: boards, shoes, wax, stickers, crews.
- **Worlds**: flyers, zines, shop walls.
- **Type**: multiple mismatched oversized fonts, ink bleeds.
- **Fingerprints**: halftone, photocopy grit, spray drips, off-register print.
- **Ratios**: 4:5, 2:3.

### Scribbles
- **Refuses**: the untouched, sacred image.
- **Heroes**: a photo (often B&W portrait) attacked by paint, crayon, marker.
- **Type**: frantic handwriting.
- **Fingerprints**: strokes that follow contours then escape into "happy accidents".
- **Ratios**: 1:1, 4:5.

### Index / Catalog
- **Refuses**: chaos.
- **Heroes**: 4–8 isolated objects that tell a story together.
- **Worlds**: solid off-white or neutral background, invisible grid.
- **Type**: small technical labels (FIG., NO., SPECIMEN), precise placement.
- **Fingerprints**: soft individual shadows; one tiny imperfection that reveals the curator.
- **Ratios**: 16:9, 3:2.

## Inventing a new trend

1. **Name it** in 2–3 words, ideally a paradox (*Tender Concrete*, *Cold Archive*, *Domestic Uncanny*, *Sunday Brutalism*).
2. **Write its refusal** in one line.
3. **Set the dials** (volume, order, reality, hand, era, distance — see `style-dna.md`).
4. **Define 4 levers**: light, signature material, type treatment, fingerprint.
5. Write the prompt with the 9 beats.

## Useful blends

- Americana × Grunge → honest rural documentary with analog damage.
- Direct Flash × Distorted Wide Angle → party chaos, phone-memory aesthetic.
- Index × Surrealism → a calm catalog where one object is impossible.
- Future Medieval × Cyber Goth → heraldic chrome armor.
- Sensory × Americana → slow, warm, tactile rural stillness.
- Scrapbook × Skate Shop → midnight zine energy.

# Model adapters

The TROP paragraph is the same everywhere. Only the wrapper changes. Model versions move fast: always use the current version of the tool the user names.

## Midjourney
- Full paragraph, then parameters: `--ar W:H --v [current]`.
- `--style raw` → more photographic honesty, less house "beautification". Recommended for Direct Flash, Grunge, Americana.
- `--stylize` low (50–150) for documentary trends, higher for Maximalism / Cyber Goth / Surrealism.
- `--chaos 10–30` when exploring a series.
- `--no` for exclusions (e.g. `--no watermark, extra text`).
- Text rendering is decent on short words; keep the word short and in quotes.

## ChatGPT (GPT Image)
- Natural language only, no `--` parameters.
- State the format in words: "vertical 4:5 format", "square format", "wide 16:9 format".
- Very good at typography and layout: put the exact text in quotes, give font style, color, size relationship and placement.
- It can follow precise layout instructions (upper third, lower left corner, along the edge).
- Inside ChatGPT, the prompt can be rendered right away; iterate by asking for one change at a time.

## Gemini (Nano Banana family)
- Natural language. State the format in words.
- Strong at edits: you can generate, then ask to "add the scanner-drag artifacts", "warp the type along the lens".
- Good text rendering; keep words short.

## Flux
- Natural language, front-load the subject and the medium in the first sentence.
- No MJ parameters; aspect ratio is set in the interface or API.
- Good text rendering; quotes around the word.

## Ideogram
- The best choice for typography-led images (Skate Shop, Scrapbook, Index, posters).
- Turn *Magic Prompt* off to keep the TROP voice intact.
- Set aspect ratio in the interface.

## Seedream / Freepik / other natural-language models
- Same paragraph, no parameters, format in words.

## Video start frames (Kling, Runway, Veo, Hailuo, Luma, PixVerse…)
- Write the still with the 9 beats.
- Add one final line: `Motion: …` describing camera move + what changes (e.g. "Motion: slow push-in as the steam drifts across the word, neon flickers once").
- For full film shot lists, pair with a dedicated filmmaking skill.

## Format cheat sheet

| Intent | Ratio |
|---|---|
| Editorial, Instagram feed, portrait poster | 4:5 |
| Cover, icon, square poster, album-style | 1:1 |
| Cinematic, banner, keynote, landscape | 16:9 |
| Story, reel, phone wallpaper | 9:16 |
| Classic photo print | 3:2 |
| Tall poster, book cover | 2:3 |
