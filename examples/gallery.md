# Gallery — new briefs, same DNA

Each prompt below was written from a fresh brief using the TROP method. None of them reuses a hero, world, word or palette from the original corpus. That's the point: **the flavor travels, the content never repeats.**

---

### 01 · Sensory Storytelling — *Awake*
**Brief:** key visual for an artisanal coffee brand.

```
A slow, smoke-heavy editorial photograph built to be smelled before it is seen. A chipped enamel mug, still steaming, sits on the scarred steel counter of an all-night roadside diner at 4 a.m. Behind it, a rain-streaked window bleeds the red neon of a motel sign into soft, wet bokeh. A single tungsten bulb carves a warm cone of light around the mug, letting the rest of the room sink into a velvety brown dark that leaves the upper third empty. Rising from that darkness, the word "AWAKE" is set in a tall, condensed slab serif, cream-colored and slightly blurred as if seen through the steam. The palette is roasted umber, oxblood red and tobacco gold. Fine 400-ISO grain and a faint fingerprint smudge on the lens keep it honest. It is a quiet ritual, not a product shot — an image that makes the viewer warm their hands before they read a word. --ar 4:5 --v 7
```
**Direction:** the coffee is never shown, only the ritual. Warm cone vs. cold night.

### 02 · Direct Flash × Distorted Wide Angle — *Now*
**Brief:** poster for a techno festival.

```
A sweat-soaked, ultra-wide fisheye photograph shot from the floor of a packed warehouse rave, looking straight up at a dancer whose outstretched hand nearly touches the lens, fingers stretched into giant, rubbery silhouettes. Above, a ceiling of steel trusses and tangled cables curves into a dizzy dome, pierced by a single green laser that slices the frame diagonally. A brutal on-camera flash freezes flying confetti and droplets of sweat mid-air while the crowd behind dissolves into motion-blurred ghosts. Bent along the edge of the lens, the word "NOW" is printed in a fat, rounded grotesque in acid lime, stretched by the same distortion as the room. The colors are toxic: chartreuse, ultraviolet and bruised magenta over deep tar black. Flash burn on the skin, a lens smear and harsh JPEG noise give it the feel of a phone photo nobody meant to keep. It is the 3 a.m. memory you can't fully remember — loud, physical, and impossible to fake. --ar 9:16 --v 7 --style raw
```
**Direction:** a blend — the flash freezes, the lens bends. The word obeys the same physics as the room.

### 03 · Surrealism — *Elsewhere*
**Brief:** luxury perfume campaign.

```
A cool, uncanny luxury photograph that feels like a dream someone forgot to finish. A woman in a stiff, ivory satin coat stands waist-deep in the perfectly still pool of a derelict 1970s spa, tiled in pale mint. Her hands cup a faceted crystal flacon from which a slow column of water rises upward, defying gravity, toward a cracked skylight. The light falls from directly above, flat and shadowless, while her reflection on the water looks the other way. Floating just above the surface, the word "ELSEWHERE" is set in a fine, high-contrast Didone serif, half-submerged so its lower edge ripples. The palette is chalk mint, pearl white and a single drop of coral. Slight medium-format softness and tiny dust specks on the print keep it analog. Nothing is explained; everything is intentional — a scent sold as a place you can't quite locate, inviting the viewer to stay inside the question. --ar 4:5 --v 7
```
**Direction:** one impossible element (rising water), one wrong reflection. Luxury through unease, not shine.

### 04 · Index / Catalog — *Still Human*
**Brief:** keynote visual for an AI conference.

```
A calm, forensic catalog layout that treats the history of human tools like museum specimens. On a warm, bone-white background, eight objects are aligned on an invisible grid: a flint arrowhead, a brass compass, a punched card, a cracked smartphone, a hand-sharpened pencil, a vacuum tube, a seed, and a single human fingerprint inked on paper. Each object sits in its own soft island of shadow, lit from a high north-facing window. Tiny monospaced captions, "SPECIMEN 01" to "SPECIMEN 08", are placed with surgical precision, and along the bottom the words "STILL HUMAN" run in a quiet, widely-tracked grotesque. The palette is limestone, graphite and one stroke of signal orange on the fingerprint. A faint paper tooth and one slightly misaligned caption betray the hand that arranged it. It turns a technological moment into an act of curation, asking the viewer to read the progression — and to notice which object doesn't belong. --ar 16:9 --v 7
```
**Direction:** an AI event that refuses to show AI. The fingerprint is the punchline.

### 05 · Scrapbook × Skate Shop — *Worn*
**Brief:** streetwear sneaker drop.

```
A shouting, photocopied zine page for a sneaker drop that looks like it was assembled on a kitchen floor at midnight. A battered, mud-splashed high-top is photographed in harsh xerox black and white, cut out with blunt scissors and glued at a tilted angle over a background of torn bus tickets, parking fines and a folded city map. Hot coral and cobalt risograph layers print slightly off-register, leaving ghost edges around every shape. Across the top, the word "WORN" is stamped in mismatched rubber-stamp letters of different sizes, the ink running dry on the last one. Hand-drawn ballpoint arrows point to the scuffs like evidence. The palette is hot coral, cobalt and newsprint grey. Staple holes, a coffee ring and scanner glare are left in on purpose. It celebrates the shoe after the shoe — the dirt as proof of a life — and makes perfection look boring. --ar 4:5 --v 7
```
**Direction:** a launch that sells the *used* product. The dry-ink letter is the human error.

### 06 · Invented trend: Tender Concrete — *Lived*
**Brief:** visual for an architecture studio.

```
A tender, sun-bleached architectural photograph where brutalism learns to breathe. A raw concrete housing block from the 1960s is shot head-on in late-afternoon light, its rigid grid of balconies softened by drying laundry, potted lemon trees and a single orange curtain flying out of an open window. A child's chalk drawing climbs the base of the wall. Long, warm shadows fall diagonally across the façade, leaving a clean, empty band of sky across the top. In that band, the word "LIVED" is set in a heavy geometric grotesque in soft concrete grey, almost camouflaged by the sky. The palette is warm concrete, terracotta, lemon yellow and faded cobalt. Gentle 6x7 film halation around the highlights and a soft vignette keep it human. It argues that buildings are only finished once people mess them up — a monument rewritten by everyday life. --ar 4:5 --v 7
```
**Direction:** a brand-new trend. Refusal: the empty, photogenic architecture shot. Levers: late sun, concrete + domestic clutter, camouflaged type, film halation.
