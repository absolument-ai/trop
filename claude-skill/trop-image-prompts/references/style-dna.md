# TROP Style DNA — annotated

This file explains *why* TROP prompts work, beat by beat, so the flavor can be recreated on any brief without copying a single original.

## 1. Two dissections

### "Direct Flash" (original 1.1), annotated

| Beat | Original wording | What it teaches |
|---|---|---|
| Frame | "A raw, high-impact editorial photograph" | 2 charged adjectives + medium + genre (editorial). Sets the stance immediately. |
| Hero | "a rebellious youth in a glossy black vinyl trench coat" | Character + one precise material. Vinyl = reflects flash = the light will "bite". |
| Light | "harsh 35mm point-and-shoot direct camera flash" | The light names a *device* and an *era*, not just a direction. |
| World | "gritty, stained concrete wall in a dark alleyway" | Texture with history (stains). |
| Light as layout | "…leaving a natural void for typography" | Light clears space for the word. Composition is designed, not left to chance. |
| Palette | "violently saturated… realistic, unpolished texture" | Intensity adverb + anti-polish note. |
| Word | `"PULSE 2026"` in "bold, sterile white sans-serif" in the corner | Clinical type vs. raw image = tension. Time-stamp = trend-report now-ness. |
| Fingerprint | "found-photo", "subtle red-eye", "slight lens flare" | Imperfections that prove a real camera. |
| Why | "lived-in, anti-studio, authentically human… before any message is processed" | Definition by negation + viewer timing. |
| Params | `--ar 4:5` | Editorial / social portrait format. |

### "Sensory Storytelling" (original 1.3), annotated

| Beat | Original wording | What it teaches |
|---|---|---|
| Frame | "An intimate, tactile macro photograph designed to evoke an immediate physical sensation before a single word is read" | The intent is *in* the frame sentence. |
| Hero | "heavy, condensation-covered emerald glass" | Weight + temperature + gem-color. Three senses in five words. |
| World | "incredibly soft, cream-colored pleated silk" | Soft vs. cold glass = tactile tension. |
| Light | "hazy, diffused morning glow that suggests warmth and comfort" | Light → emotion, explicitly. |
| Fingerprint | "crisp water droplets frozen in detail… 'feel' the coldness" | Scare quotes on the sensory verb. |
| Word | `"BREATHE"`, "thin, elegant, widely-spaced serif", lower third | Typography mirrors the mood (quiet), placement given. |
| Why | "a whisper, not a shout… a slight pause and a deep emotional connection" | Paradox pair + viewer effect. |

**Lesson:** loud trends and quiet trends share the same skeleton. Only the dials change.

## 2. The dials

Every beat is a dial. A TROP prompt sets them deliberately:

- **Volume**: whisper ←→ shout (Sensory vs. Skate Shop)
- **Order**: catalog grid ←→ total collage (Index vs. Maximalism)
- **Reality**: documentary ←→ impossible (Americana vs. Surrealism)
- **Hand**: machine-clean ←→ visibly handmade (Index vs. Scribbles)
- **Era**: archaic ←→ futuristic (Future Medieval spans both)
- **Distance**: macro intimacy ←→ fisheye aggression

Choosing a new combination of dial positions is how you invent a trend.

## 3. Vocabulary families (inspiration, not a checklist)

Use these as *families* to find your own words. Do not paste the same terms in every prompt.

- **Frame adjectives**: raw, high-impact, hyper-dense, intimate, tactile, dynamic, gritty, high-concept, quiet, narratively heavy, meticulously ordered, loud, dark, mystical, fine-art.
- **Materials**: vinyl, velvet, faux fur, enamel, pleated silk, chrome, rough-hewn wood, peeling wallpaper, newsprint, wax crayon, tech-wear, obsidian, polaroid, scotch tape.
- **Light sources**: point-and-shoot flash, morning haze, golden hour, invisible multiple sources, flat overhead, tungsten bulb, north window, neon spill.
- **Color as matter**: obsidian, molten orange, acid green, electric blue, burnt sienna, sage, faded ochre, canary yellow, acid violet, electric cyan, emerald, cream.
- **Fingerprints**: film grain, scratches, chemical artifacts, light leaks, scanner dust, motion drag, halftone, ink bleed, off-register print, chromatic aberration, red-eye, lens flare, JPEG noise, staple holes, coffee ring.
- **Type treatments**: liquified chrome, circular warp along the lens, distressed typewriter, ink-smudged, geometric blackletter, brutalist block, sterile sans, widely-spaced serif, mismatched oversized fonts, frantic handwritten scrawl, technical labels ("FIG. 04").
- **Why-verbs**: forces the viewer to linger, pulls the audience into, rewards the viewer for, creates a pause, sparks curiosity, signals humanity, reconnects with.

## 4. Generic vs. TROP

**Generic prompt**
> A beautiful cup of coffee on a table, cozy atmosphere, warm lighting, 8k, ultra detailed, masterpiece.

**TROP prompt**
> A slow, smoke-heavy editorial photograph built to be smelled before it is seen. A chipped enamel mug, still steaming, sits on the scarred steel counter of an all-night roadside diner at 4 a.m. Behind it, a rain-streaked window bleeds the red neon of a motel sign into soft, wet bokeh. A single tungsten bulb carves a warm cone of light around the mug, letting the rest of the room sink into a velvety brown dark that leaves the upper third empty. Rising from that darkness, the word "AWAKE" is set in a tall, condensed slab serif, cream-colored and slightly blurred as if seen through the steam. The palette is roasted umber, oxblood red and tobacco gold. Fine 400-ISO grain and a faint fingerprint smudge on the lens keep it honest. It is a quiet ritual, not a product shot — an image that makes the viewer warm their hands before they read a word. --ar 4:5 --v 7

What changed: a stance (ritual, not product shot), a material hero (chipped enamel), a world with history (all-night diner), light that composes (cone + empty upper third), a word in tension with the dark, colors as matter, a fingerprint, a closing why.

## 5. Rhythm

- Sentences of mixed length. One long descriptive sentence, then a short verdict.
- Present tense, declarative. No "please", no "I want".
- The last sentence lands like a tagline.
