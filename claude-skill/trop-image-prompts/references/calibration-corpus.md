# Calibration corpus — the original TROP prompts

> **Calibration only. Never reuse, reword or reassemble these prompts.** They exist so the voice can be measured: rhythm, density, beat order, the closing "why". Every new prompt must have a new hero, a new world, a new word and a new palette.

Original author: Jérémy Gross — *Design trend report 2026*. Section names keep the original French in parentheses.

## Tendances Photographiques

### 1.1 Direct Flash (Flash Direct)

```
A raw, high-impact editorial photograph of a rebellious youth in a glossy black vinyl trench coat, captured with a harsh 35mm point-and-shoot direct camera flash. The subject stands against a gritty, stained concrete wall in a dark alleyway. The lighting creates a sharp, aggressive separation between brilliant highlights and deep, obsidian shadows, leaving a natural void for typography. The colors are violently saturated, with skin tones reflecting a realistic, unpolished texture. Overlaid in the corner is the text "PULSE 2026" in a bold, sterile white sans-serif font. The image has a "found-photo" aesthetic with a subtle red-eye effect and a slight lens flare. It feels lived-in, anti-studio, and authentically human, capturing a split-second moment of raw urban life before any message is processed. --ar 4:5 --v 6.0
```

### 1.2 Maximalism (Maximalisme)

```
A hyper-dense, maximalist visual explosion rejecting all principles of clean design. The frame is a claustrophobic collage of clashing textures: leopard print velvet, neon pink faux fur, and iridescent digital glitch patterns. In the center, a 3D chrome typography reads "LOUD" in a liquified, distorted font. The background is a sensory overload of vintage 90s magazine clippings, dried tropical flowers, and scattered gold coins. Colors are cranked to extreme saturation—electric blues, acid greens, and molten oranges. There is no white space; every pixel is filled with detailed subjects and eclectic clutter that somehow maintains a hidden thematic hierarchy. It is a daring, extreme version of curation that forces the viewer to linger and decode the organized chaos. --ar 1:1 --v 6.0
```

### 1.3 Sensory Storytelling (Récit Sensoriel)

```
An intimate, tactile macro photograph designed to evoke an immediate physical sensation before a single word is read. The focus is on a heavy, condensation-covered emerald glass resting against a backdrop of incredibly soft, cream-colored pleated silk. The lighting is a hazy, diffused morning glow that suggests warmth and comfort. Small, crisp water droplets are frozen in detail, making the viewer "feel" the coldness and the humidity of the scene. In the lower third, the word "BREATHE" is placed in a thin, elegant, widely-spaced serif font that creates intrigue. This is a whisper, not a shout, using visual cues of texture and temperature to create a slight pause and a deep emotional connection with the audience. --ar 4:5 --v 6.0
```

### 1.4 Distorted Wide Angle (Grand Angle Distordu)

```
A dynamic, low-angle ultra-wide fisheye lens photograph of a person mid-air, jumping over a metallic railing. The urban architecture in the background—curving skyscrapers and brutalist stairs—is warped and stretched by the lens distortion, creating a dizzying 90s music video vibe. The subject is positioned extremely close to the glass, making their sneakers look unnaturally large and imposing. Overlaid "MOTION" text is warped in a circular path, hugging the lens curvature. The colors are cool-toned with a high-fashion, grainy film look. It creates an instant, aggressive presence that pulls the audience directly into the action, utilizing stretching and perspective warping to guide the eye through a dynamic, unexpected layout. --ar 16:9 --v 6.0
```

### 1.5 Grunge

```
A gritty, low-fidelity black and white photograph capturing an honest, unwashed presentation of reality. The subject shows visible dirt under the fingernails and messy, sweat-dampened hair, rejecting hyper-polished commercial standards. The background is a dark, industrial basement with peeling wallpaper and exposed wiring. The image is heavy with film grain, scratches, and chemical artifacts from an analog process. In the center, "STAY REAL" is scrawled in a distressed, ink-smudged typewriter font. There is a deliberate energy of friction and imperfection that gives the design a human pulse. It feels found, chaotic, and deeply authentic, acting as a signal of humanity in a world of perfected digital manufacturing. --ar 4:5 --v 6.0
```

### 1.6 Surrealism (Surréalisme)

```
A high-concept surrealist photograph that feels familiar but fundamentally "wrong". A person in a sharp, minimalist suit stands in a vast, empty salt flat under a midnight-blue sky. Instead of a head, they hold a large, floating, glowing iridescent bubble that reflects an impossible forest landscape. The word "VISION" floats in the air in a liquified liquid-chrome typeface. The lighting is uncanny, with shadows cast from multiple invisible sources, creating a provocative subtext. This is a human-crafted concept, full of soul and intention, designed to spark curiosity and push the design into unexpected, thought-provoking territory. It creates a level of connection impossible to achieve with generic, automated artificial creation. --ar 1:1 --v 6.0
```

### 1.7 Americana

```
A quiet, narratively heavy photograph capturing the raw grit and resilience of the American West. A weathered cowboy hat sits on a rough-hewn wooden fence overlooking a vast, dusty prairie during the golden hour. The background features the faint silhouette of a small rural town under a sky filled with hanging dust particles. The imagery is honest and non-romanticized, showing the real toughness of the land. Centered is the word "HONEST" in a heavy, rigid brutalist block font that contrasts with the natural texture. The colors are earthy—burnt siennas, sage greens, and faded ochres. It evokes deep emotion and narrative weight, connecting with the audience through a story of people and culture grounded in a quiet, powerful stillness. --ar 16:9 --v 6.0
```

## Tendances De Design

### 2.1 Scrapbook / Scanner

```
A complex, handmade design composition that feels "human first, digital second". The layout consists of jagged, hand-cut paper edges, vintage stamps, and polaroids held together by strips of semi-transparent scotch tape. The background is a bright canary yellow paper with visible fiber texture and scanner bed dust. Large "2026" text is intentionally distorted, appearing as if it were dragged across a flatbed scanner while the lid was open, creating light leaks and blurred motion artifacts. Minor errors, lack of precision, and overlapping shadows showcase the evidence of creation by hand. It is a messy, layered collage that rejects pixel-perfection in favor of unpredictability and a distinctly human-made quality that cannot be perfectly recreated digitally. --ar 4:5 --v 6.0
```

### 2.2 Cyber Goth

```
A dark, aggressive visual language merging futuristic aesthetics with the emotional weight of goth culture. The center features intricate, 3D chrome spikes and sharp line work reminiscent of digital tribal markings. The background is a dense, obsidian void filled with digital noise, distortion, and harsh chromatic aberration. Angular, sharp typography reads "NEO" in an iridescent silver, shimmering with toxic hyper-colors like acid violet and electric cyan. It is a bold, anti-establishment hero of a trend, packed with attitude and controlled chaos. The design has "teeth," feeling raw and emotionally charged, successfully moving from underground scenes into a high-fashion, high-impact advertising context without losing its provocative, harsh edge. --ar 1:1 --v 6.0
```

### 2.3 Future Medieval (Médiéval Futuriste)

```
A dark, mystical fusion of the Middle Ages and digital futurism. A modern figure in sleek, obsidian tech-wear is adorned with intricate, glowing orange gothic engravings and ornamental detailing. The layout is strictly symmetrical, bordered by ornate gargoyles and mythical griffins. In the center, "KINGDOM" is written in a sharp, geometric Blackletter calligraphy that feels both archaic and digital. The background is a textural, dark forest shrouded in mist, adding a layer of depth and emotional richness. It is a visually rich, symbolic aesthetic that rejects tech-focused minimalism. The vibe is strange yet familiar, reconnecting the audience with ancient storytelling through a digital lens, creating a design that feels like a mythical artifact from the year 2026. --ar 4:5 --v 6.0
```

### 2.4 Skate / Surf Shop

```
A loud, chaotic design flyer that "shouts" with a total rejection of grids and structure. The layout is a messy mix of halftoned photographs of surfboards and skateboards, layered with neon spray paint drips and gritty photocopy textures. The colors are a clashing, high-vibrancy palette of hot yellow and electric blue. Large, bold text "REBEL" uses multiple mismatched, oversized fonts with visible ink bleeds and newsprint halftone effects. It feels like a character-rich, custom piece created through an analog process of printing, cutting, and scanning. This is a rebellious, anti-polish aesthetic that celebrates mistakes and brings fun back into design, challenging the digital chokehold of modern perfection. --ar 4:5 --v 6.0
```

### 2.5 Scribbles (Gribouillages)

```
A fine-art mixed-media piece that blurs the line between professional design and raw art. A moody, black and white fashion portrait is aggressively scrawled over with bright red oil paint strokes and messy wax crayon doodles. The scribbles follow the contours of the subject's face but often veer off into abstract "happy accidents". These little texture notes and imperfections clearly show the artist's hand. The text "SO IT GOES" is handwritten in a frantic, ink-heavy scrawl in the corner. The design screams humanity and craft, utilizing color balance and high contrast to cause a pause. It is a tactile, crafted final product that feels alive, intentional, and deeply personal, rewarding the viewer for spending time with the work. --ar 1:1 --v 6.0
```

### 2.6 Index / Catalog

```
A meticulously ordered, clinical design layout that invites the viewer to browse and discover. On a solid, clean off-white background, a grid of high-resolution cutout objects is arranged: a single leather boot, a smooth river stone, a mechanical watch movement, and a dried leaf. Each element is isolated with minimal, soft shadows, claiming its own space within a structured whole. Small, technical sans-serif labels like "FIG. 04" are placed precisely next to each item. The layout forms a narrative from smaller components, offering a sense of calm and clarity in a chaotic world. It is part catalog, part collage, showcasing curation and taste through a presentation that makes complex visual ideas easy to absorb and understand. --ar 16:9 --v 6.0
```
